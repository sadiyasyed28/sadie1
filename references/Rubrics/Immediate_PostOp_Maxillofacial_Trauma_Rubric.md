# Immediate Post-Operative Maxillofacial Trauma Rubric

**Source:** User-supplied PDF, `Rubric & assessment guide_Maxillofacial_Trauma_Immediate_PostOp` (9 pages; Google Sheets export).  
**Project:** ADRAB / SADIE maxillofacial trauma treatment-outcome evaluator.  
**Status:** **SURGEON REVIEW REQUIRED - NOT APPROVED FOR CLINICAL SCORING.**  
**Purpose of this file:** Structured transcription and software-mapping reference. The supplied PDF remains the source document; this Markdown file is not an independent clinical guideline or validation.

## 1. Source structure

The guide contains:

1. A master all-fracture-type, three-tier rubric.
2. A point-of-care immediate post-operative patient assessment and audit form (worked example).
3. A streamlined bedside post-operative checklist for PACU to Day 3 rounds.
4. Fracture-specific immediate post-operative assessment factors and three-tier rubrics for six fracture groups.

The three grades are described in the source as:

- **Grade 3 - Optimal / Satisfactory**
- **Grade 2 - Suboptimal / Monitor**
- **Grade 1 - Unsatisfactory / Revision**

The source does not provide a separately defined grade for not assessed, not applicable, indeterminate, or conflicting evidence.

## 2. Master rubric (source page 1)

| Fracture type | Assessment domain | Grade 3 - Optimal / Satisfactory | Grade 2 - Suboptimal / Monitor | Grade 1 - Unsatisfactory / Revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mandibular | Clinical: Occlusion | Restoration of premorbid Angle Class I / pre-injury intercuspation without premature contacts. | Minor occlusal disharmony or minimal step-off correctable with guiding elastics. | Severe malocclusion, anterior open bite, or gross dental step-off requiring revision. |
| Mandibular | Clinical: Function & V3 | Pain-free TMJ opening; intact or improving inferior alveolar/mental nerve sensation (V3). | Transient hypesthesia in V3 distribution; mild TMJ stiffness without mechanical locking. | Complete V3 anesthesia; expanding sublingual hematoma or acute airway compromise. |
| Mandibular | Radiological: Reduction | Complete cortical reduction along inferior border (<1 mm gap); condylar head centered in fossa. | Minor cortical gap (1-2 mm); condylar head slightly eccentric but contained within fossa. | Displaced reduction gap (>2 mm), condylar dislocation, or screw penetration into tooth roots. |
| ZMC & arch | Clinical: Contour & mouth opening | Symmetrical malar projection from suprabrow view; smooth coronoid mouth opening without trismus. | Mild malar projection asymmetry (<2 mm); minor trismus resolving with physical therapy. | Marked cheek flattening/retrusion; severe trismus due to persistent coronoid impaction. |
| ZMC & arch | Clinical: Neuro-ocular | Intact baseline infraorbital nerve sensation (V2); no diplopia in primary gaze. | Mild transient cheek hypesthesia (V2); no ocular motility restriction. | Persistent diplopia, retrobulbar hematoma, or severe infraorbital nerve transection. |
| ZMC & arch | Radiological: 4-point buttress | Anatomic realignment across all 4 articulations (ZF, ZM, ZS greater wing, arch) on 3D CT. | Satisfactory alignment across 3 key buttresses with minor residual arch gap (<2 mm). | Persistent displacement or rotational malalignment across multiple buttress joints. |
| Orbital | Clinical: Vision & motility | Normal visual acuity and light reflexes; full extraocular motility without entrapment or diplopia. | Mild diplopia at extreme peripheral gazes; extraocular tracking preserved. | Relative afferent pupillary defect (APD), acute vision drop, muscle entrapment, or severe diplopia. |
| Orbital | Clinical: Globe position | Symmetrical globe position; Hertel exophthalmometry within <2 mm of contralateral orbit. | Mild globe displacement (<2 mm enophthalmos/hypoglobus) without visual restriction. | Marked enophthalmos or hypoglobus (>2 mm asymmetry) or proptosis with tense orbit. |
| Orbital | Radiological: Mesh & volume | CT confirms complete orbital volume restoration; mesh/plate anchored securely on posterior ledge. | Minor residual orbital volume enlargement; reconstructive implant stable. | Reconstructive mesh mispositioned, unanchored, or entrapment of orbital fat/rectus muscle. |
| Le Fort midface | Clinical: Stability & bite | Rigid, immobile maxilla on manual palpation; restored pre-injury centric occlusion and height. | Minor maxilla-mandible occlusal discrepancy correctable with guiding elastics; stable midface. | Gross maxillary mobility ("floating maxilla/palate"), dish-face deformity, or open bite. |
| Le Fort midface | Clinical: Airway & CSF | Airway completely patent; absence of active CSF rhinorrhea or nasal bleeding. | Transient bloody nasal discharge resolving spontaneously; non-obstructed airway. | Active clear CSF rhinorrhea (halo sign / beta-2 transferrin +) or nasal airway collapse. |
| Le Fort midface | Radiological: Buttresses | Continuous rigid fixation along vertical (NM, ZM) and horizontal buttresses on CT. | Buttresses realigned with minor bone gaps (<2 mm) bridged by hardware. | Unreduced pterygomaxillary junction displacement, widespread buttress collapse, or plate failure. |
| NOE | Clinical: Canthal & ICD | Normal intercanthal distance (ICD 30-35 mm); sharp medial canthal angle without telecanthus. | Slightly widened ICD (35-37 mm) without frank rounding of medial palpebral fissure. | Traumatic telecanthus (>37 mm ICD), rounded medial canthus, or avulsed medial tendon vector. |
| NOE | Clinical: Nasal & lacrimal | Restoration of dorsal nasal projection; uncompromised nasolacrimal drainage. | Mild dorsal nasal asymmetry; transient epiphora without infection. | Dorsal nasal collapse, active nasolacrimal duct obstruction, or septal hematoma. |
| NOE | Radiological: Central fragment | CT confirms anatomical reduction and secure transnasal wire fixation of central fragment. | Central fragment aligned with minor surrounding ethmoidal comminution. | Displaced or unreduced central fragment with severe lacrimal fossa disruption. |
| Frontal & panfacial | Clinical: Contour & neuro | Smooth, continuous forehead profile; intact dural seal without CSF leak; normal CN exam. | Minor contour irregularities hidden under soft tissue swelling; no neurological deficits. | Visible forehead depression, active CSF leakage, or intracranial neurological deterioration. |
| Frontal & panfacial | Radiological: Sinus walls | Full reduction of anterior sinus wall; posterior wall/dural integrity verified; NFOT patent/obliterated. | Anterior wall realigned with minor fragment gaps (<2 mm); posterior wall intact. | Displaced posterior wall fracture with pneumocephalus, dural tear, or NFOT obstruction. |
| Frontal & panfacial | Radiological: Panfacial tiers | Sequential alignment across all facial tiers (Mandible -> Midface -> Frontal bar) restoring proportions. | Minor localized asymmetry in non-load-bearing areas; main skeletal height/width restored. | Panfacial collapse, loss of vertical facial height, or uncoordinated tier alignment. |

Abbreviations are retained from the source: ZF, zygomaticofrontal; ZM, zygomaticomaxillary; ZS, zygomaticosphenoid; NM, nasomaxillary; NOE, naso-orbito-ethmoid; NFOT, nasofrontal outflow tract; ICD, intercanthal distance; APD, relative afferent pupillary defect; TMJ, temporomandibular joint; IMF, intermaxillary fixation.

## 3. Point-of-care audit form (source page 2)

The form includes patient/surgical metadata, an assigned grade (1-3) for each feature, an evaluated outcome level, and a free-text clinical observations/findings field. Its ten example features are:

1. Airway & sublingual soft tissue (clinical)
2. Dental occlusion & bite (clinical)
3. Pupillary reflex & APD screen (clinical)
4. Extraocular motility & diplopia (clinical)
5. Cranial nerve function (V2/V3/VII) (clinical)
6. CSF leak & hemorrhage control (clinical)
7. Facial symmetry & skeletal stability (clinical)
8. 3D CT buttress alignment (radiological)
9. CT orbital volume & mesh position (radiological)
10. Hardware adaptation & cortical gap (radiological)

**Important:** The example contains a named sample patient, sample clinical findings, grades, a total of 29/30, a mean of 2.90, and an "OPTIMAL / PASS" outcome. These are worked-example values only. They must never be used as patient data, reference labels, ground truth, or proof that the aggregation method is clinically approved.

## 4. Bedside checklist (source page 3)

The source labels this checklist for **PACU to Day 3 rounds**.

| Core feature | Bedside examination method | Normal / expected finding | Warning sign / red flag | Immediate action / escalation stated in source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Airway & sublingual tissue | Palpate floor of mouth; inspect tongue height and sublingual mucosa. | Soft, non-tender floor of mouth; midline tongue; uncompromised breathing. | Firm, tense sublingual hematoma; tongue elevation; stridor/dyspnea. | Immediate airway consult; bedside decompression; prepare for re-intubation. |
| Subjective & objective bite | Ask whether bite feels normal; inspect tooth contact and IMF. | Reproducible pre-injury centric occlusion; flush arch bars/elastics. | Anterior open bite; premature molar contact; lateral step-off. | Re-apply IMF elastics; obtain immediate OPG/CT; plan occlusal revision if rigid. |
| Pupillary reflex & APD | Swinging flashlight test; assess pupil size, symmetry, and gross vision. | Brisk direct and consensual reflexes bilaterally; equal pupils; APD negative. | Sluggish/fixed pupil; APD positive (Marcus Gunn pupil); acute vision loss. | Stat ophthalmology consult; emergent orbital CT; prepare for lateral canthotomy. |
| Extraocular motility | Ask patient to follow finger in 9 cardinal gazes (H-pattern). | Smooth, full tracking in all gazes; zero diplopia in primary gaze. | Gaze restriction (vertical/horizontal); persistent primary-gaze diplopia. | Coronal/axial orbital CT; check for muscle entrapment/screw impaction. |
| Cranial nerves (V2/V3/VII) | Check cheek (V2), lip/chin (V3) light touch; test smile and eye closure (VII). | Intact or baseline sensation; symmetric smile and tight eye closure. | New complete V3/V2 anesthesia; complete facial nerve branch drop. | Document baseline; check plate location relative to nerve canals on post-op CT. |
| CSF leak & hemorrhage | Lean patient forward; inspect nose/ears for clear fluid (halo test). | Dry surgical sites; absence of clear watery nasal/ear discharge. | Clear watery rhinorrhea/otorrhea (Halo sign +); expanding facial swelling. | Send fluid for beta-2 transferrin; elevate head of bed 30 degrees; avoid nose blowing; neurosurgery consult. |
| Symmetry & stability | Observe from "bird's-eye" view; gently palpate zygomas and maxilla. | Symmetrical cheek prominence; rigid immobile midface and palate. | Malar flattening; gross maxillary mobility ("floating palate/maxilla"). | Obtain 3D CT reconstruction; plan open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) revision if unreduced. |

The action language above is copied as a transcription of the supplied checklist, not independently validated or a substitute for local emergency protocols or clinician judgment.

## 5. Fracture-specific assessment factors and rubrics (source pages 4-9)

### 5.1 Mandibular fracture (page 4)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Dental occlusion: restoration of pre-injury intercuspation without premature contacts or step-offs.
- IMF & arch bar stability: intact intermaxillary fixation wires/elastics maintaining tight dental closure.
- TMJ mobility & range: passive/active interincisal opening without joint locking or acute deviation.
- Inferior alveolar / mental nerve: baseline sensory check of lip and chin anesthesia/hypesthesia (V3 distribution).
- Floor of mouth & airway: absence of expanding sublingual hematoma, tongue elevation, or airway compromise.

**Radiological assessment factors (CT / OPG)**
- Cortical border alignment: anatomical reduction along inferior border (source criterion states gap <1-2 mm).
- Occlusal plane realignment: leveling of occlusal plane on panoramic/CT views.
- Condylar head positioning: centering within glenoid fossa without displacement.
- Hardware & nerve canal: bicortical/monocortical screw placement clear of inferior alveolar canal and tooth roots.

**Three-tier parameters**
- Clinical occlusion & bite: Grade 3 Angle Class I intercuspation restored and stable bite; Grade 2 minor occlusal step-off correctable with elastics; Grade 1 severe malocclusion or open bite requiring revision.
- Nerve & airway status: Grade 3 intact/improving V3 sensation and soft floor of mouth; Grade 2 transient V3 hypesthesia and mild sublingual edema; Grade 1 V3 anesthesia or expanding hematoma/airway compromise.
- CT cortical & condylar reduction: Grade 3 cortical gap <1 mm and condyle centered; Grade 2 gap 1-2 mm and condyle slightly eccentric; Grade 1 gap >2 mm, condylar dislocation, or root screw impaction.

### 5.2 ZMC & zygomatic arch (page 5)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Malar projection & symmetry: visual and palpation comparison of cheek prominence from suprabrow view.
- Coronoid clearance & trismus: smooth mouth opening without coronoid impaction against zygomatic arch.
- Infraorbital nerve sensation: sensory baseline of upper lip, lateral nose, and anterior gingiva (V2).
- Gross ocular motility: absence of extraocular muscle restriction or diplopia in primary gaze.

**Radiological assessment factors (3D CT)**
- 4-point buttress reduction: alignment across ZF, ZM, ZS (sphenoid wing), and arch articulations.
- Orbital rim contour: smooth infraorbital and zygomaticomaxillary rim reduction without step-offs.
- Zygomatic arch projection: symmetrical bow and projection on axial CT.
- Fixation plate stability: secure screw engagement at ZF and ZM buttresses.

**Three-tier parameters**
- Malar contour & trismus: Grade 3 symmetrical cheek projection and unhindered opening; Grade 2 mild cheek asymmetry (<2 mm) and minor trismus; Grade 1 cheek flattening or severe trismus from coronoid impaction.
- Infraorbital nerve (V2): Grade 3 intact/baseline sensation; Grade 2 mild transient cheek numbness; Grade 1 complete V2 anesthesia or retrobulbar hematoma.
- 3D CT 4-point buttress alignment: Grade 3 anatomical reduction across ZF, ZM, ZS, and arch; Grade 2 alignment across 3 buttresses with arch gap <2 mm; Grade 1 multi-buttress displacement or rotational malalignment.

### 5.3 Orbital floor, medial wall & trapdoor (page 6)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Pupillary reflex & APD screen: immediate check for Marcus Gunn pupil / APD to rule out retrobulbar hematoma.
- Extraocular muscle motility: absence of muscle entrapment; smooth vertical/horizontal tracking.
- Diplopia evaluation: absence of binocular diplopia in primary and reading gazes.
- Hertel exophthalmometry: symmetrical globe position (<2 mm asymmetry relative to contralateral orbit).

**Radiological assessment factors (CT)**
- Orbital volume restoration: compare orbital cavity volume (cm³) with uninjured orbit.
- Reconstructive mesh position: mesh/plate rests securely on stable posterior and medial bony ledges.
- Soft-tissue decompression: release of herniated periorbital tissue; no trapped rectus muscle.

**Three-tier parameters**
- Vision & extraocular motility: Grade 3 normal acuity/pupils, full motility, no diplopia; Grade 2 mild peripheral gaze diplopia with normal pupils; Grade 1 APD positive, vision loss, rectus entrapment, or severe diplopia.
- Globe position (Hertel): Grade 3 symmetrical globe position (<2 mm difference); Grade 2 mild displacement (<2 mm enophthalmos/hypoglobus); Grade 1 marked enophthalmos/hypoglobus (>2 mm) or tense proptosis.
- CT mesh & volume restoration: Grade 3 complete volume restoration and mesh on posterior ledge; Grade 2 minor volume discrepancy with stable implant; Grade 1 mispositioned/unanchored mesh or fat/muscle entrapment.

### 5.4 Le Fort midface (page 7)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Midface manual stability: physical stability on manual palpation of maxilla (no mobility).
- Occlusal relationship: correct maxilla-mandible orientation with reproducible centric occlusion.
- Facial height & projection: restoration of vertical facial proportions; absence of dish-face retrusion.
- CSF rhinorrhea clearance: absence of active clear nasal discharge (halo sign / beta-2 transferrin negative).

**Radiological assessment factors (multiplanar CT)**
- Vertical buttress realignment: continuity of nasomaxillary and zygomaticomaxillary buttresses.
- Horizontal & pterygoid alignment: realignment of infraorbital rim and pterygomaxillary junction.
- Rigid plate fixation: stable plate adaptation across load-bearing midface pillars.

**Three-tier parameters**
- Maxillary stability & bite: Grade 3 rigid maxilla and restored centric occlusion; Grade 2 minor discrepancy correctable with elastics; Grade 1 floating maxilla/palate, dish-face deformity, or open bite.
- CSF rhinorrhea & airway: Grade 3 patent airway and no CSF leak; Grade 2 transient bloody nasal discharge resolving; Grade 1 active clear CSF rhinorrhea or airway collapse.
- CT buttress realignment: Grade 3 continuous vertical/horizontal buttress alignment; Grade 2 aligned buttresses with minor gaps (<2 mm); Grade 1 pterygomaxillary collapse or widespread plate failure.

### 5.5 Naso-orbito-ethmoid (NOE) (page 8)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Intercanthal distance (ICD): measurement between medial canthi (source states normal 30-35 mm).
- Medial canthal tendon vector: intact posterior-superior tendon vector; sharp medial palpebral fissure.
- Nasal projection & profile: restoration of dorsal nasal prominence and projection.
- Nasolacrimal duct patency: absence of epiphora or tear duct obstruction.

**Radiological assessment factors (fine-slice CT)**
- Central fragment reduction: reduction of tendon-bearing central bone fragment.
- Transnasal canthopexy wire: secure anchor/positioning of wire for central fragment.
- Lacrimal fossa integrity: structural integrity without fragment intrusion.

**Three-tier parameters**
- Intercanthal distance: Grade 3 ICD 30-35 mm and sharp medial canthal angle; Grade 2 ICD 35-37 mm without frank rounding; Grade 1 traumatic telecanthus (>37 mm) or rounded medial canthus.
- Nasal & lacrimal status: Grade 3 restored dorsal nasal height and normal tear drainage; Grade 2 mild asymmetry and transient epiphora; Grade 1 dorsal nasal collapse or active lacrimal duct obstruction.
- CT central fragment & wire: Grade 3 anatomical reduction with transnasal wire; Grade 2 central fragment aligned with minor comminution; Grade 1 displaced central fragment or lacrimal fossa disruption.

### 5.6 Frontal sinus & panfacial trauma (page 9)

**Clinical assessment factors**
- Forehead contour & profile: smooth anterior sinus wall profile without depression or step-off.
- Dural seal & CSF clearance: absence of forehead wound CSF leakage or rhinorrhea.
- Neurological baseline: full sensorium check and cranial nerve screen (CN I-VI).
- Panfacial proportions: restoration of overall facial height, width, and depth.

**Radiological assessment factors (CT)**
- Anterior/posterior sinus walls: reduction of anterior wall and verification of posterior wall integrity.
- Nasofrontal outflow tract (NFOT): confirm patency or complete sinus obliteration.
- Sequential tier realignment: bottom-up/top-down tier continuity (Mandible -> Midface -> Upper).

**Three-tier parameters**
- Forehead contour & dural seal: Grade 3 smooth profile, no CSF leak, normal CN exam; Grade 2 minor irregularity under swelling and no neurological deficit; Grade 1 visible depression, active CSF leak, or neurological decline.
- CT sinus wall & NFOT: Grade 3 anterior wall reduced and posterior wall/NFOT intact or obliterated; Grade 2 anterior wall realigned with minor gaps (<2 mm); Grade 1 displaced posterior wall, pneumocephalus, or NFOT obstruction.
- CT panfacial tier realignment: Grade 3 sequential alignment across Mandible-Midface-Upper tiers; Grade 2 minor localized non-load-bearing asymmetry; Grade 1 panfacial collapse or uncoordinated tier alignment.

## 6. Software integration contract (ADRAB)

This source should be treated as the immediate-post-operative, fracture-specific rubric candidate. It is not interchangeable with the repository's older `data/phase_05_candidate_rubric.csv` or the existing eight-domain long-term treatment-outcome evaluator.

### 6.1 Allowed work before surgeon approval

- Store and display the source rubric and its version/status.
- Implement non-clinical case administration, authentication/authorization, secure file intake, metadata capture, audit trails, and workflow state handling.
- Capture source-aligned observations as raw, attributed data: clinical observation, examination method, imaging modality/view, measurement value/unit, assessor, date/time, and source file/reference.
- Build a non-scoring checklist UI that can show the source's assessment factors and red-flag wording as reference material, provided it is clearly labelled as a draft surgeon-review rubric and not used to diagnose or grade.
- Preserve the distinction between clinical and radiological evidence.
- Provide missing/unknown/not-assessed states in data capture without assigning a grade.

### 6.2 Disabled until surgeon approval

- Assigning Grade 1, 2, or 3 automatically or manually as an ADRAB clinical outcome.
- Deriving grades from CT/CBCT/X-ray measurements or AI model outputs.
- Calculating totals, means, weights, percentage scores, or an overall PASS/FAIL.
- Training or evaluating a clinical prediction model against this rubric.
- Presenting a result as a treatment recommendation, revision decision, or declaration of successful healing.

### 6.3 Safety and provenance requirements

- Red flags must be represented as distinct safety events/escalation flags, not merely as low rubric grades or items that can be averaged away.
- Missing, unassessed, inapplicable, poor-quality, and conflicting evidence must remain explicitly distinguishable; none may be silently converted to normal findings or Grade 3.
- Keep clinical examination, patient-reported information, and radiological observations in separate provenance channels.
- Imaging-derived measurements must include modality, acquisition/view, units, measurement method, quality limitations, and human reviewer where applicable.
- Do not infer a 3D measurement from a 2D image. Do not infer ocular, airway, neurological, occlusal, or other clinical findings from imaging alone.
- All displayed AI assistance must cite the exact case evidence/source used and state uncertainty/limitations. The AI must not invent measurements or clinical observations.
- The source's emergency-action wording must not replace institution-specific protocols or clinician judgment.

### 6.4 Surgeon decisions required before activating scoring

1. **Aggregation conflict:** Page 2 shows a 29/30 total, mean 2.90, and "OPTIMAL / PASS". The source does not define validated aggregation cutoffs or explain how an overall pass should be derived. Confirm whether only per-criterion grades are intended or whether a total/mean/outcome is approved.
2. **Grade mapping:** Confirm whether the three grade labels and descriptors apply consistently to every fracture type and assessment parameter.
3. **Not-assessed / not-applicable handling:** Define explicit states and whether a case can be summarized when one or more criteria cannot be assessed.
4. **Timing:** Confirm the intended assessment window. The bedside checklist explicitly says PACU to Day 3; the remaining sections use "immediate post-op" without a single timing definition.
5. **Multiple fracture patterns:** Define how to select and combine fracture-specific sections in combined/panfacial injuries.
6. **Threshold clarification:** Review numerical boundaries and overlapping wording. For example, the orbital globe-position section describes both Grade 3 symmetry and Grade 2 mild displacement using a "<2 mm" boundary. Confirm the exact interpretation.
7. **Imaging protocols:** Confirm modality, slice thickness, planes, measurement technique, quality requirements, and who verifies each radiological criterion.
8. **Emergency escalation:** Confirm local escalation pathways and who is responsible for acknowledging and documenting critical red flags.
9. **Approval record:** Surgeon(s), specialty reviewers, approval date, rubric version, changes requested, and effective date.
